Introduction

Learning that asking does not make us less worthy of love.

There are people who will give you everything they can, but struggle to ask you for one small thing.

They will listen, encourage, show up, pray, help carry the weight, and make room for the needs of others. Yet when their own moment comes, the words become difficult. They rehearse the request. They apologize before anyone has answered. Sometimes they decide not to ask at all.

Why is receiving so uncomfortable for us? Why can a simple request feel like an admission of weakness, or a burden placed on someone we love?

Perhaps we have confused humility with silence. Perhaps we have learned to measure our worth by how little we need. Or perhaps we have experienced relationships where asking was met with resentment, reminders, or shame.

But grace is not only demonstrated by the person who gives. Grace is also present in the person who receives with gratitude, in the person who listens without judgment, and in the person who can say no without cruelty.

Throughout these reflections, we will consider how love makes room for honest needs while still respecting responsibility and boundaries. We will look at the ways guilt can distort our relationships and how the example of Jesus calls us toward compassion that is both truthful and generous.

This is not an invitation to demand from others. It is an invitation to stop treating every need as a failure. You are human. You will have moments when you need help. And someone else will, too.

May we learn to give without keeping score, receive without shame, and love without making people feel small for being human.

You are not a burden because you need grace. You are human, and grace was made for people.

Bradley Hobbs
