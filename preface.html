The author's reflection on why The Grace of Asking was written.

Preface

This book began with a simple observation about something many of us do without giving it much thought: we apologize for asking.

We say, “I’m sorry to bother you,” or “I’m sorry for asking,” even when the request is reasonable and the person we are approaching has given us no reason to believe we are an inconvenience. Sometimes the person receiving the request is perfectly willing to help, yet the person asking still feels the need to apologize.

That small interaction led me to consider a much larger question. Why do we sometimes struggle to receive the same kindness we are so willing to extend to others?

As I began exploring that question, I realized that this was not simply about asking someone to do something. It was about relationships, personal responsibility, communication, boundaries, forgiveness, and the way we understand grace. It was about recognizing that we can become so concerned with not inconveniencing others that we forget there is nothing inherently wrong with having needs.

I also wanted to examine the other side of the conversation. Being willing to help someone does not mean we must fulfill every request. Love should never become an obligation that leaves one person carrying everything while the other forgets personal responsibility. There must be room for consideration, honest answers, and the freedom to say yes or no without making someone feel guilty.

My hope is that these pages encourage honest reflection rather than judgment. I am not writing from a position of having every relationship figured out or every lesson mastered. Like everyone else, I recognize that understanding grace requires us to examine our own attitudes, responses, and expectations.

Most importantly, I want readers to consider whether they are extending to themselves the same grace they so readily offer others.

If this book helps you reconsider one unnecessary apology, receive one act of kindness without guilt, or approach someone you love with greater understanding, then its message has served a purpose.

Grace is something we must learn to give, but it is also something we must learn to receive.

Let grace go both ways.
